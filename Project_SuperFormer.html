01 · Problem

Accuracy at a cost

The question

Can a model find the main object in an image as well as large models do, while being small and fast enough for phones and robots?

Pixels

Tens of thousands of tiny squares: sharp, but costly to process.

Downsampled

Fewer, bigger squares: cheaper, but the edges get blurry.

Superpixels

A few hundred irregular pieces that follow the edges: cheap and sharp.

Background

Salient object detection finds the most visually prominent objects in an image, supporting faster image retrieval, robotics and detection of objects without predefined labels. Most research has chased accuracy with ever larger networks, which slows inference and rules out resource-constrained devices.

Lightweight models usually save effort with compact feature extractors. Reducing the spatial size of the input itself, the other big driver of computation, had remained largely unexplored.

Superpixels group neighbouring pixels with similar colour and texture into regions, shrinking the input from tens of thousands of pixels to a few hundred segments while keeping object boundaries far better than plain downsampling.

The catch is that superpixels are heterogeneous: they vary in shape, size, rotation and location, so they don't fit the regular grids standard networks expect. SuperFormer is designed around that heterogeneity.

02 · Proposed solution · Method

Describing irregular regions

SuperFormer describes every superpixel by three things, so a transformer can work with pieces of any shape, size or position.

  • ColourThe colour and texture inside the piece.
  • ShapeIts outline as a few Fourier waves; the same when the piece is moved, resized or rotated.
  • PositionWhere its centre sits in the image, through a dynamic centroid positional embedding (DCPE).

How it works

  1. 1SegmentSplit the image into a few hundred superpixels with SLIC.
  2. 2DescribeGive each piece its colour, shape and position.
  3. 3EncodeSWIN Transformer encoders relate the pieces to each other.
  4. 4DecodeMix-attention decoders turn them into a saliency map.

It comes in three sizes: SF-XXS (0.14M parameters), SF-XS (1.12M) and SF-S (2.18M).

03 · Findings

Lighter and better

Tested on five natural-scene datasets (DUTS-TE, DUTS-O, ECSSD, HKU-IS and PASCAL-S) and two aerial ones, each SuperFormer size is the most accurate in its class, and the fastest.
