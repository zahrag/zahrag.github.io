01 · Problem

Learning to drive by seeing

The question

Can a small, probabilistic agent learn to drive from a compact summary of what the camera sees, and keep driving well when that summary comes from real camera images instead of the simulator?

Background

Autonomous vehicles must turn what they see into steering and speed decisions. Deep reinforcement learning can learn this from trial and error, but it needs huge amounts of experience and is hard to interpret; imitation learning needs expert demonstrations.

This project asked whether a compact, probabilistic learner could do the job: a Bayesian reinforcement learning agent that drives in the CARLA simulator from a small summary of the scene, and that can move from virtual to real-world inputs.

Autonomous driving spans many levels, from route planning down to low-level control; this work learns the step from perception to control signals. Model-free deep reinforcement learning has mastered games and control tasks, but neural networks need vast amounts of interaction data and are sensitive to hyperparameters.

Simulators such as CARLA make safe training possible, but what is learned in simulation must survive the move to the real world; mapping camera images to a semantic segmentation narrows that gap. Imitation learning needs expert demonstrations, which rarely exist for dangerous situations, and maximum-entropy methods become unstable when rewards and entropy are scaled poorly. This project builds instead on a Bayesian approach to reinforcement learning.

02 · Proposed solution · Method

Bayesian reinforcement learning

The agent models what it perceives with a Gaussian mixture: each mixture component represents a kind of situation, and a learned value table links components to actions. For each new state it picks the action most likely to be right given the components that state resembles.

How it works

  1. 1SeeTurn the camera image into a segmentation map with five classes.
  2. 2SummarizeCut the map into six regions and measure each class in each region.
  3. 3MatchFind which kinds of situation, the parts of a Gaussian mixture, the view resembles.
  4. 4ActA learned value table picks the action most likely to be right: forward, left, right or reverse.

Rewards and actions

Collision

The strongest penalty.

Off-road and wrong lane

Penalties scaled by how far the car strays.

Speed

Reward for keeping close to a target speed.

Four actions

Forward, left, right and reverse, mapped to steering, throttle and brake.

03 · Findings

Ground truth and estimates

The agent is trained in one CARLA town, Town01, in sunny weather, on a straight road and on right and left turns, without intersections or other road users. Nine models are trained for each setting.

  1. Ground truth (G): the segmentation comes straight from the simulator, clean.
  2. Estimated (E): the segmentation is predicted from the camera image by EncNet, with errors, as in a real car.
  3. T and D: T is the input used in training, D the input used while driving. TGDE means trained on ground truth, driving on estimates.

04 · My role
