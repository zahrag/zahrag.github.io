01 · Problem

When the scene changes

The question

Can a model that forecasts where pedestrians will walk keep working in a place it has never seen, by learning what stays the same everywhere?

Background

Motion forecasting models learn from pedestrian trajectories recorded in particular places. Move them to a new environment, with different crowds, layouts or walking styles, and their predictions often break down, because they have learned correlations that were specific to the training data.

Some factors that shape motion hold everywhere, such as physical laws, traffic rules and social norms. Others change from place to place, such as the motion styles of the people there. A model that keeps these apart can carry what is universal into new domains.

Causality in brief

Structural causal models

Directed acyclic graphs where an edge X → Y means changing X can change Y, but not the reverse.

Confounders

Variables that influence both cause and effect, creating misleading associations.

Backdoor criterion

A set of variables that, controlled for, blocks confounding paths so p(Y | do(X)) can be estimated.

Causal representation learning

Learning latent variables that capture causal structure, not only correlations.

02 · Proposed solution · Method

Separating the invariant from the variant

GCRL is a generative model built on a causal graph. It learns two hidden factors from past paths: one that stays the same everywhere, and one that depends on the place.

  • E
    EnvironmentWhich place the data comes from. It sets the style of that place.
  • S
    Variant features, latentWhat changes from place to place, such as walking style.
  • Z
    Invariant features, latentWhat holds everywhere: physical laws, traffic rules, social norms.
  • X
    Past pathThe observed trajectory so far.
  • Y
    Future pathThe trajectory to predict.

Filled circles are observed; dashed circles are hidden and learned by the model.

Both Z and S influence the past and the future, so they confound the link between X and Y. Using the backdoor criterion, the model estimates the true causal effect p(Y | do(X)) rather than a correlation that may not transfer.

How it works

  1. 1EncodeRead the past path of each pedestrian.
  2. 2SeparateInfer the invariant part Z and the variant part S.
  3. 3PredictGenerate the future path from the past, Z and S.
  4. 4AdaptIn a new place, keep Z and tune only S.

One objective, five goals

  1. Accurate forecasts, by maximizing the likelihood of the true future trajectories.
  2. No confounding, by estimating the causal effect of X on Y, p(y | do(x)).
  3. Faithful encoding, by reconstructing the past trajectories from the latents.
  4. Invariant representations, by pulling the posterior of Z towards its prior.
  5. Variant representations, by matching the posterior of S to its environment-specific prior.
03 · Findings

Steady under shift

Tested on real crowds (ETH-UCY: 1,536 pedestrians in five scenes) and on synthetic crowds in eight domains, GCRL holds up where other forecasters break down.
