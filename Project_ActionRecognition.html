01 · Problem

Seeing actions as they happen

The question

Can a system learn to recognize actions such as waving or running from body movement alone, without labels, while they happen?

Animated illustrations of actions from my studies; the motions are drawn by code, not recorded data. Hover or tap a tile to see its name.

Background

Waving, catching, lifting, running: people recognize actions effortlessly and continuously, without anyone labelling the start and end of each one. Machines usually need labelled, pre-cut examples.

This project, which grew out of my PhD in Cognitive Science at Lund University, asked how a system could learn actions the way the brain might: from the dynamics of the body, self-organizing without supervision, and recognizing actions online as they unfold.

In Johansson's experiments (1973), an actor dressed in black was filmed against a black background, with small reflecting patches on the main joints. About ten moving dots were enough: observers recognized walking or jogging after only about two hundred milliseconds. Yet when the film was stopped, nobody saw a body in the still dots. Only the motion carries the action.

Johansson explained this with visual vector analysis. Dots that move together are seen as the ends of rigid rods, the bones. The brain subtracts the common motion of the trunk, the shoulders and hips, and what remains are the pendulum swings of the knees and elbows and the turns of the wrists. These patterns are what we recognize.

Runesson and Frykholm called this the kinematic specification of dynamics: the movement of the dots carries the forces behind it. Observers can tell how heavy a lifted box is even when the box is invisible, how strenuous push-ups are, whether someone only pretends to paint a wall, and even the gender of a walker.

Perception works in levels: first the motion patterns of single joints, then whole actions and their goals. The order of the key postures matters too. Lift the arm and push it forward, and it is a punch or a throw; take the reverse path, pull the arm back and lower it, and it is a catch.

Building on Gärdenfors's theory of conceptual spaces, actions can be represented by these underlying force patterns, which show up in the second-order dynamics of movement, its accelerations. Self-organizing maps, which learn topology-preserving maps of their input without labels, much like maps in the cortex, offer a natural way to learn such patterns.

02 · Proposed solution · Method

One system, six steps

The same core runs through all the studies: a depth camera tracks the body, the joints and their movement are read, and layers of self-organizing maps learn the actions without labels. Each study then adds one thing.

The core

  1. 1Kinect inputReceive and prepare live depth-sensor data.
  2. 23D jointsExtract the skeleton and how each joint moves.
  3. 3Self-organizing mapsLayers of maps learn action patterns, with no labels.
  4. 4RecognitionName the action, online, as it happens.

From positions to forces

Position

Where the joint is, frame by frame.

Velocity

How fast it moves, and in which direction.

Acceleration

How its movement changes: the force behind it.

MSRAction3D, generalization test

Recognition accuracy on test data the system had not learned from; higher is better. Without attention to the body parts most involved, accuracy was about 70%.

  1. Objects (step 3): the system recognized 91.1% of actions (push, pull, put, lift and point) and identified the object acted on, among four, in 100% of cases, by combining the action stream with a proximity measure.
  2. Intention (step 6): internally simulated perception improves recognition in all experiments, and lets the system continue an action when the input is cut short.

Growing grids against maps (step 5)

Growing grid networks beat self-organizing maps of the same size on every dataset, and learn three to four times faster.

04 · My role

What I built

  1. Designed and implemented the action recognition system: Kinect input, 3D joint extraction and recognition.
  2. Developed the methods: hierarchical self-organizing maps, growing grid networks and online recognition of unsegmented streams.
  3. Validated the pipeline on MSRAction3D, UTKinect and Florence3DActions.
  4. Contributed as a researcher to the multi-partner projects WYSIWYD, Ikaros and Thinking in Time.
